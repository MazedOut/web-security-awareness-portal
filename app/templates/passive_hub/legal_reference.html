Legal Reference: IT Act 2000

A simplified overview of key sections in the Indian Information Technology Act, 2000 regarding cybercrimes.

Key Sections & Punishments

Offence: Accessing, downloading data, introducing viruses, or damaging a computer system or network without the owner's permission.
Penalty: Compensation up to Rs. 1 Crore to the affected person.

Offence: Doing any act referred to in Section 43 dishonestly or fraudulently (e.g., hacking).
Penalty: Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to Rs. 5 Lakh, or both.

Offence: Dishonestly receiving or retaining any stolen computer resource or communication device.
Penalty: Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to Rs. 1 Lakh, or both.

Offence: Fraudulently or dishonestly making use of the electronic signature, password, or any other unique identification feature of any other person.
Penalty: Imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to Rs. 1 Lakh.

Offence: Cheating by impersonating someone else using a computer resource or a communication device (e.g., phishing).
Penalty: Imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to Rs. 1 Lakh.

Offence: Intentionally capturing, publishing, or transmitting the image of a private area of any person without their consent.
Penalty: Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to Rs. 2 Lakh, or both.

Offence: Publishing or transmitting obscene material in electronic form.
Penalty: First conviction: Imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to Rs. 5 Lakh. Second conviction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to Rs. 10 Lakh.

Offence: Securing access to electronic records, books, or registers without consent and disclosing such information to any other person.
Penalty: Imprisonment up to 2 years or fine up to Rs. 1 Lakh, or both.

Offence: Corporate body possessing sensitive personal data fails to implement reasonable security practices, causing wrongful loss or gain.
Penalty: Liable to pay damages by way of compensation to the affected person.

Your Digital Rights

Under the IT Act and Indian laws, you have specific rights in the digital realm:

  • Right to Privacy: Recognized as a fundamental right. Protection against unauthorized access to your data.
  • Right to Compensation: Under Section 43A, if a company loses your sensitive data due to poor security, you can seek compensation.
  • Protection against Cyber Stalking: Covered under IPC Section 354D, providing protection to women against digital monitoring and stalking.
  • Legal Recognition of E-Docs: Digital signatures and electronic contracts hold legal validity.
